Flow:

Real Estate Development Process Flow
1. Land / Property Acquisition
The first step is acquiring the land or existing property. The acquisition can be completed either through cash from investors or through bank financing.

If the acquisition is financed, the bank may structure the land/property purchase and construction financing together as one loan package, similar to the structure used for 392 1st Street.

If the acquisition is completed in cash, the purchase funds are contributed by the investors, similar to 161 Woodlawn Avenue. In this scenario, the construction loan is obtained at a later stage.

2. Survey, Due Diligence & Closing
A property survey and other required due diligence are completed as part of the acquisition process. Once all closing requirements are satisfied, the property is purchased and ownership is transferred to the project entity.

3. Architectural Plans & Township Submission
Once the property is acquired, the architect begins preparing the construction and development plans. The completed plans are then submitted to the township or municipality for review and approval.

This design, submission, and approval process typically takes approximately four months.

4. Construction Financing – If Property Was Purchased With Cash
If the land or property was originally purchased using investor cash, this architectural and municipal approval period is also when the project begins the process of obtaining a construction loan from the bank.

The goal is to have the financing ready so construction can begin once the necessary approvals and permits are obtained.

5. Construction Loan Closing & Permits
Once the construction loan is approved, the loan closing is completed. At approximately the same stage, the final permits and municipal approvals are obtained so the project can move into construction.

6. Construction Begins
After financing and permits are in place, construction begins.

The bank funds the project through a construction draw process. Funds are released progressively based on completed construction milestones.

Typical milestones may include site work, foundation, framing, roofing, mechanical systems, plumbing, electrical, insulation, drywall, finishes, and final completion.

7. Construction Completion
Construction continues through all required milestones until the project is substantially complete. Final inspections, certificates of occupancy, and any other required municipal approvals are then obtained.

8. Broker Listing & Marketing
As construction approaches completion, a listing agreement is signed with the real estate broker. The condominium units are then listed and marketed for sale.

9. Unit Sales & Closings
Buyers enter into contracts for the individual condominium units. Each buyer completes their financing, appraisal, attorney review, and closing requirements.

The units are then sold and closed individually.

Overall Flow:
Land Acquisition → Survey & Closing → Architectural Plans → Township Submission → Construction Financing → Loan Closing & Permits → Construction → Bank Draws → Construction Completion → Broker Listing → Unit Sales → Unit Closings
